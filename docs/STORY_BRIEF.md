# Woke & Rooted — Editorial companion (draft, not findings)

**Working title:** *The Geography of Being Unwell: Where Distress Meets Disconnection*

**Central question:** What can public-health estimates teach us about the gap between visible community life and reported social/emotional well-being?

**Reporting path:**
1. Ground the opening in the CDC's definition of frequent mental distress (14+ of the last 30 days).
2. Explain why three measures were selected (distress, isolation, lack of support).
3. Insert only *verified* results from `data/report.csv`, with data vintage and missingness.
4. Seek local voices and service providers before drawing interpretation or suggesting actions.
5. Ask what supportive institutions—including congregations—can do without presuming faith involvement is measured.

**Questions for future reporting:** What barriers separate social contact from felt emotional support? How do community-based organizations invite and listen without stigmatizing? What services already exist locally?

**Do not claim:** that religion causes/protects against poor mental health; that these estimates identify who is struggling; that a flagged county is clinically or morally deficient; that correlations represent causes.

**Source credit:** CDC PLACES, County Data 2024 release (predominantly 2022 BRFSS source data), dataset fu4u-a9bh.
